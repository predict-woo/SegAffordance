# Survey: point-track / flow prediction as a manipulation interface (2023-2026)

Scope: how the field parameterises, supervises and smooths *predicted future point tracks* (2D/3D), read against our 20-point relative-trajectory MLP head that zigzags. Facts below were checked against the arXiv HTML of each paper on 2026-09-10; "n/s" = not stated in the paper.

## What the field actually does

1. **Per-point, whole-horizon regression heads are still common and are not considered wrong**: ATM, 3PoinTr (linear head), General Flow (2-layer MLP), PointWorld and GeoPredict (shared MLP), FLIP (MLPs for scale/direction). None of them reports a roughness/jitter metric, and none ablates a temporal decoder against a per-point MLP. So the claim "the field converged on a temporal decoder" is **not supported**; what is supported is that the heads that *look* smooth in their figures either (a) predict deltas, (b) use robust losses, or (c) put time on a sequence axis inside a generative model.
2. **Time as a sequence axis is the dominant choice whenever a generative model is used**: video-style diffusion (Im2Flow2Act, 3DFlowAction, JOPAT), latent VAE + flow matching (What Happens Next), autoregression (ARM4R, AMPLIFY, TAPNext, Magma, Myriad: "causal over time and, within each step, over trajectories"), and iterative refinement with temporal conv/attention (TAPIR, CoTracker3). Diffusion Policy's argument is the relevant one: single-step heads "alternate between two valid trajectories, resulting in jittery actions"; predicting the chunk jointly commits to one mode.
3. **Relative/delta parameterisation wins where ablated**: FLIP (delta vs absolute), General Flow (TLN normalisation: scale L and direction predicted separately, best ADE 3.58 in the ablation), AMPLIFY (velocities), Myriad (incremental Δx with tanh-saturated Fourier embedding). Our head already predicts positions relative to the first point but not per-step deltas.
4. **Robust, visibility-masked losses are universal in trackers** (Huber/L1 in TAPIR, CoTracker3, TAPNext, 3PoinTr, PointWorld, What-Happens-Next) — and our 2D-only targets (hand-keypoint tracks resampled to 20 points) are exactly the kind of noisy supervision they were designed for.
5. **Explicit first-difference losses** (MADiff/Uni-Hand angle + length losses) exist but are always used *on top of* a diffusion model; no paper shows them fixing jitter of a plain MLP, consistent with our "wash" result.

## What applies to our head (ranked)

1. **Time-query decoder (1-2 layers) instead of a 60-d MLP output.** 20 learned time queries q_t = e_t + PE_sin(t) (GeoPredict), self-attention over the 20 queries + cross-attention to the (spatial, not pooled) image/condition features, shared linear head → Δ or position; this is the CoTracker3/TAPIR "temporal attention over track tokens" pattern applied to forecasting. Why it reduces jitter: adjacent outputs come from nearly identical queries through the same weights, so the head cannot fit per-index target noise independently; smoothness is learned, not imposed by a basis. Side effects: needs the un-pooled feature map to be worth it; can over-smooth corners (drawer stop) if capacity is tiny; ~0.5-1 M params. Caveat: no direct ablation vs per-point MLP in the literature.
2. **Delta parameterisation with a separate scale head (FLIP/General Flow).** Output unit direction d_t (20×3) and one scalar total length L (or per-step δ_s ≥ 0), p_t = Σ_{s≤t} L·Δn_s with Σ‖Δn_s‖=1. Loss on the cumulated positions (General Flow's L_acc) plus L_scale on 3D data only. Why: magnitude jitter collapses into one scalar; the 2D projection loss then mostly shapes direction. Side effects: error accumulation over 20 steps (mitigated by L_acc); scale is unobservable from 2D-only data, so keep the scale head supervised only on 3D batches. Combine with 1 by letting the time-query decoder emit Δn_t.
3. **Residual refinement with a depthwise temporal conv (TAPIR/CoTracker3).** Keep the MLP as the coarse initialiser; add 1-2 blocks of Conv1d(k=5, depthwise) + pointwise MLP over [coarse p_t, PE(t), image feature bilinearly sampled at the projected p_t]; predict residuals; supervise every iteration with weights γ^{M−m} (γ=0.8). Why: the residual path with a local temporal receptive field is a data-dependent smoother and gets image evidence at the projected location, which the pooled vector lacks. Side effects: extra forward passes; requires the differentiable projection you already have.
4. **Robust, masked supervision.** Replace L2 in the 2D projection loss by Huber (thr ≈ 6 px, CoTracker3) or L1 (3PoinTr), mask indices where the hand keypoint is occluded/low-confidence (TAPIR weights Huber by (1−ô_t)), and low-pass the hand track *before* resampling to 20 points. Why: L2 on noisy index-matched targets rewards chasing per-index noise; Huber caps its gradient. Cheapest change; expected effect modest and not quantified anywhere in the literature.
5. **Causal / generative head (Myriad, ARM4R, AMPLIFY, MT-π).** A 20-step GRU/causal-transformer emitting Δ_t conditioned on p_{t−1}, or a small flow-matching head over the 20×3 chunk. Strongest coherence and the only fix for multimodal targets (Diffusion Policy's mode-alternation argument), but the 2D projection loss must then be applied through x0-prediction, and sampling costs grow; rank last on cost.

Note on 2D-only supervision: the projection loss leaves the depth coordinate of each of the 20 points unconstrained on hand-video batches, so z-jitter is invisible to the loss; options 1-3 all couple z across time through shared weights, which is the main reason they should help beyond the visible x-y zigzag.
