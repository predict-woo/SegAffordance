# Trajectory-parameterization survey — how modern work gets smooth predicted trajectories

2026-08-21, three parallel code-level literature sweeps (egocentric/affordance,
motion forecasting, articulated-object + robot policy). Motivation: our
20×3 relative trajectory head (direct MLP readout) is noisy/non-smooth;
delta+cumsum helped somewhat. Question: what does modern research use?

## The one-sentence answer

Nobody makes a single-shot MLP emit long raw waypoint sequences and hopes:
every 2022–2026 line imposes structure via one of four mechanisms —
(1) analytic rollout from articulation/screw parameters, (2) truncated
low-frequency basis coefficients, (3) first-difference (velocity)
supervision on point heads, (4) sequence-level generative priors
(diffusion/flow) — and the choice tracks how much geometric structure the
task has.

## Family 1 — analytic rollout from articulation parameters
**Dominant in articulated-object work — and our task IS articulated.**

- **FlowBot++** (CoRL'23, arxiv 2306.12893): predicts per-point flow +
  projection-to-axis, decodes axis/origin analytically, then rolls out the
  K-step trajectory in closed form — revolute via Rodrigues
  `R(ϕ)=I+sinϕ[ω]ₓ+(1−cosϕ)[ω]ₓ²`, prismatic via linear ray; type branch =
  classifier gating two closed-form decoders. Replan every 7 steps instead
  of 1; 17.1s → 1.2s per object.
- **Ditto** (CVPR'22): parameters only (dense per-point, median/RANSAC
  aggregation); motion generated by articulating the twin. **PARIS**
  (ICCV'23): interpolates the transform parameter. No trajectory head
  exists in either.
- **ScrewMimic** (RSS'24, arxiv 2405.03666): screw S=(q,ŝ,h) with pitch h
  unifying rot (h=0) and trans (h→∞) — no hard type branch; waypoints =
  `exp([S]θk)·T₀`. (This is our abandoned twist parameterization,
  vindicated at the decode side.)
- Smoothness: perfect by construction. Noise control: dense prediction +
  robust aggregation, never a single regression.

## Family 2 — truncated low-frequency basis (motion-forecasting standard)

- **DCT coefficients** (Mao ICCV'19 → siMLPe WACV'23 → HumanMAC ICCV'23 →
  essentially all successors): head emits K coefficients per axis, decode
  = one fixed `idct_m[:, :K]` matmul, **loss on decoded points, never on
  coefficients**, plus a **velocity (first-difference) L2 aux loss at
  weight 1.0** (siMLPe: 3 lines of code, SOTA with a 0.14M-param MLP —
  exactly our head class). Typical compression 4–6×: HumanMAC keeps 10–20
  coeffs for 60–125 frames ⇒ for our 20 points, **K≈5–8**.
- **Bézier control points** (BezierLaneNet CVPR'22): cubic (4 points),
  Bernstein matrix decode, L1 on sampled points. PLOP: degree-4
  polynomials.
- Caution (**MultiPath++**, Waymo): very-low-degree global polynomials can
  underfit and lose to raw waypoints once multimodality is handled — DCT
  with tunable K is the safer basis.
- Smoothness: band-limited by construction — jitter is unrepresentable.

## Family 3 — first-difference supervision on point heads (cheapest)

- **VAT-Mart** (ICLR'22, doors/drawers!): waypoints stored as residuals
  (deltas) "as it empirically yields better performance" — direct
  published support for our delta+cumsum observation.
- **USST** (ICCV'23): absolute-point head PLUS a velocity head, tied by a
  warp loss (`last_obs + cumsum(pred_velo) ≈ pred_traj`) and a velocity
  loss vs `diff(gt)`; worth +4.8cm generalization.
- **MADiff** (2024): angle loss `1−cos(Δpred,Δgt)` + length loss
  `|‖Δpred‖−‖Δgt‖|` on consecutive-segment vectors — direction and speed
  supervised separately from position.
- **GeneralFlow** (CoRL'24): deltas + cumsum + direction/scale
  decomposition + accumulation loss on the integrated curve.

## Family 4 — sequence-level generative priors

Diffusion/flow over whole future sequences (Diff-IP2D, MADiff, Track2Act,
im2Flow2Act, Diffusion Policy, π0) or chunking + inference-time overlap
averaging (ACT temporal ensembling, Real-Time Chunking). No explicit
smoothness losses anywhere — the prior does it. Overkill for a 20-point
single-shot head.

## What this means for us (SegAffordance, gen-17 era)

Our GT trajectories are SYNTHESIZED perfect screws (90° arcs at the true
radius / 0.7m rays) — the analytic-rollout family can represent our GT
with ZERO error, and we already predict its inputs (type, axis, origin via
the split heads). Three graded options, all compatible with the relative
frame:

1. **Cheap patch (no architecture change):** add siMLPe's velocity loss
   (w=1.0) + MADiff's angle/length losses on the current head. Hours of
   work, proven, bounded upside.
2. **Field-standard head swap:** DCT-basis head — `Linear → K×3` (K≈6),
   fixed `idct_m` buffer decode, losses unchanged on decoded points +
   velocity aux. Smooth by construction, keeps the trajectory head as an
   INDEPENDENT evidence pathway (which the gen-9 supervision ablation
   showed matters: joint training helped both directions through L_pp).
3. **Structural endgame:** analytic screw decode — replace the 20×3 head
   with a scalar magnitude; decode 20 points via Rodrigues (rot head's
   axis + lifted origin) or ray (trans head's direction), gated per row.
   Perfectly smooth, exactly matches GT's generative family, and in the
   2D-ONLY line it would route the projection data term's gradient
   DIRECTLY into axis/origin/magnitude — potentially fixing the
   L_pp-too-weak articulation deadlock. Costs: the trajectory stops being
   independent evidence (L_pp becomes vacuous; axis errors become
   trajectory errors verbatim), and the trans/rot decode branch needs the
   predicted-type gating we already built for the axis heads.

Recommendation recorded: option 2 (DCT) as the next 3D-line experiment —
lowest risk, field-standard, preserves the measured joint-training
benefit; option 3 as the high-upside experiment for the 2D-only line,
where its gradient-routing property attacks the known articulation
deadlock. Option 1's losses can ride along with either.
