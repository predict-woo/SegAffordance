# Survey v2 — Point-track / flow prediction as a manipulation affordance: what head structures the literature uses, and what beats a plain MLP

*Clean-slate literature survey, 2026-09-10. Scope: methods that predict future 2D/3D point tracks (or dense flow) from a single image (+ language / goal) for manipulation, plus the parameterisation and refinement machinery of point-tracking backbones. Written against the SegAffordance setting: one RGB image + instruction -> mask, contact point, articulation type/axis/origin, and a 20-point scale-free 3D trajectory of the interaction point, currently read out by a tiny MLP on a pooled 512-d fused vector (plain 20x3, or 6 DCT-II coefficients/axis).*

## 0. TL;DR

1. **Nobody in this field regresses a whole trajectory from a single pooled vector with a bare MLP.** Even the "regression" family (ATM, Tra-MoE, Gen2Act, ManiTrend, 3PoinTr) uses *per-point / per-timestep query tokens* that attend to *spatial* image features, with a linear/MLP readout per token. The pooled-vector MLP is the baseline everyone implicitly beats.
2. **The dominant 2024-26 head is generative**: a diffusion or flow-matching (rectified-flow / stochastic-interpolant) transformer over the set of tracks (Track2Act, FlowBotHD, 3D Flow Diffusion Policy, RoboFlow4D, Forecasting-Motion-in-the-Wild, VidBot, Diff-IP2D, EgoMAN, TraceGen), or a repurposed video generator that emits trajectories instead of pixels (Im2Flow2Act, 3DFlowAction, "What Happens Next?"). Direct evidence that this beats regression: *Affordance-FM* 1.01 cm vs 4.91 cm (transformer BC) on 3D waypoint error; *What Happens Next?* best-of-K MSE 10.99 vs 23.07 for the ATM regressor on LIBERO-90; *FlowBotHD* 95 % vs 80 % without diffusion; *Diff-IP2D* 7.9 %/13 % better WDE/FDE than autoregressive regressors.
3. **Parameterisation matters as much as the head**: scale/shape decomposition (General Flow's total-length normalisation), residual/velocity targets (siMLPe, CoTracker, Forecasting-Motion), unit-sphere or arc-length normalisation (3PoinTr, TraceGen), and low-order bases with hard boundary constraints (BEAST's clamped B-splines, FAST's DCT) all show measurable gains and, importantly, *structural* smoothness rather than learned smoothness.
4. **Point trackers refine, they do not regress once**: PIPs / TAPIR / CoTracker3 all apply 4-6 iterations of *delta* updates to positions under an exponentially-weighted L1/Huber loss; TAPIR's AJ goes 41.6 -> 55.0 -> 61.3 for 0 -> 1 -> 4 iterations. Track-On shows a classification-then-offset readout beats pure regression for precision (delta-1px 45.5 vs 27.6 without the offset head).
5. For our specific failure (OOD squiggles while mask/type/axis stay right), the best-supported fixes are (i) a **physically parameterised head** that decodes the trajectory *from* the axis/type heads plus a 1-D motion profile, (ii) a **query-token decoder with spatial cross-attention and delta refinement**, and (iii) a **small flow-matching head with best-of-K + axis-consistency guidance**. Details and costs in Section 4.

## 2. Prose by family

### 2.1 Regression track transformers (per-point query tokens, not pooled vectors)

ATM is the canonical regression baseline: the *only* thing it predicts is H=16 future absolute normalised 2D coordinates for N=32 query points, and it does so by giving each query point its own token, masking the future, and letting that token attend to image patches and BERT text tokens inside a ViT-style transformer, followed by an MLP readout per token. Tra-MoE keeps exactly this interface and swaps FFNs for top-1 MoE, which is the interesting result for us: with a dense regressor, adding *out-of-domain* co-training data hurt (57.6 -> 52.0), whereas the MoE turned the same data into a gain (51.8 -> 61.4). That is a direct data point on "regression heads over-average across domains". Gen2Act uses a 6-layer track transformer purely as an auxiliary loss on policy latents, and the only axis it materially helps is *motion-type* generalisation (5 -> 30 %) — i.e. track supervision teaches "how things move", which is the very quantity our head loses OOD. ManiTrend (3D flow as pixel x, pixel y, metric depth; masked patch tokens seeded from start coordinates, bidirectional attention, linear readout) and 3PoinTr (MLP tokenizer -> 2-block decoder -> linear readout, L1, unit-sphere normalisation) show that even lightweight regressors in 2025-26 are *token-per-point/timestep decoders* with L1 and explicit normalisation. None of these papers reports a head-vs-MLP ablation because a pooled-vector MLP is not considered a contender; the closest thing is the ATM-vs-generative comparison in "What Happens Next?" below.

### 2.2 Generative heads over track sets (diffusion / flow matching)

Track2Act was the first to treat the *set of tracks* as the thing to denoise: one token per point, a 24-block DiT, image/goal embeddings via adaLN, H=50, absolute pixels. It reports no deterministic-head ablation but is the template copied by FlowBotHD (DiT over per-point 3D flow), 3D Flow Diffusion Policy (per-point *displacement* deltas, L1 noise loss, 100 FPS-sampled points, DDIM 10 steps), RoboFlow4D (FlowDiT with v-prediction, plus an explicit Charbonnier second-difference smoothness term) and Forecasting-Motion-in-the-Wild (DiT-B over *unordered* track tokens whose positional encoding is the initial (x,y) — exactly our single-point case, N=1 is a legal input — with velocity reparameterisation and L1). The evidence that generative beats deterministic where it has been measured is consistent: FlowBotHD 95 % vs 80 % without diffusion (multi-modal doors 23 -> 77 %); Affordance-FM 1.01 cm vs 4.91 cm transformer BC; Diff-IP2D 0.411 vs 0.446 WDE against the best autoregressive regressor; "What Happens Next?" best-of-K MSE 10.99 vs 23.07 for ATM. Three caveats matter for us. (i) Most of these compare on *multimodal* data; the gain is partly from best-of-K evaluation. (ii) Affordance-FM shows flow matching > DDPM/DDIM at equal steps and that *one-step* FM is nearly as good (1.031 cm), so the compute argument against generative heads is weak. (iii) VidBot is the one paper that couples a diffusion trajectory head with *test-time structural guidance* (goal, collision, contact-normal) and ablates it: 85.6 % vs 57.8 / 73.3 / 77.8 / 74.5 without coarse goal / multi-goal / collision / cost-based sample selection. That is the pattern that maps onto our axis/type heads.

### 2.3 Video-generator-style trajectory generators

Im2Flow2Act, 3DFlowAction, TraceGen and "What Happens Next?" all reuse a video-diffusion backbone (AnimateDiff, CogVideoX, a DiT with a trajectory VAE) but emit a trajectory *grid* rather than pixels. Common design lessons: encode the initial keypoint positions with sinusoidal/Fourier features, generate the *complete* task flow rather than the next step (Im2Flow2Act ~+30 %), bypass or retrain the VAE when depth is a channel (3DFlowAction), predict velocity-like increments (TraceGen) and normalise time by arc-length (TraceGen). "What Happens Next?" also gives the cleanest statement of why trajectories are a better target than pixels (LRTL 15.9 vs 31.1 for a fine-tuned WAN 14B) and shows OOD transfer from synthetic physics to real scenes. These are 0.5-2 B-parameter models; they are relevant to us as *evidence*, not as heads.

### 2.4 Parameterisation and normalisation (mostly free, consistently helps)

* **Scale / shape decomposition.** General Flow predicts a *normalised* displacement sequence (divided by total path length L) and L separately, weighting the scale loss 25x; ablations give ~0.2 cm ADE and ~0.3 cm FDE gains and, more importantly, it is the mechanism that lets one model handle door points that travel far and body points that barely move. Our "delta / depth-of-first-point" normalises for camera distance but not for motion magnitude; a total-length (or per-axis) scale factor is the missing piece.
* **Residual / velocity targets.** siMLPe (0.14 M params, SOTA human-motion forecasting in 2022) attributes most of its gain to residual-to-last-frame prediction and an L2 *velocity* loss (111.3 -> 109.4 mm), and shows the DCT itself only gives 1 mm. CoTracker3, PIPs and TAPIR update positions by predicted deltas; Forecasting-Motion denoises velocities, not positions.
* **Low-order bases with hard constraints.** BEAST (clamped uniform B-spline, N=10 control points for 20-step chunks, first control point pinned to the previous action) beats per-step binning 4.43 vs 1.41 on CALVIN and lifts ACT from 49 to 70 % on bimanual tasks with visibly smooth curves and no chunk seams; N=5 and N=20 are both worse than N=10, matching our finding that 6 DCT coefficients over 20 steps is the right order. FAST shows DCT + quantisation is enough to make AR tokens work at 50 Hz where binning gives 0 %. B-spline Policy reports reduced jerk and 2-4x execution speed-ups with the same imitation learner.
* **Discrete vs continuous readout.** BEAST: discrete control-point tokens +12.7 % over a linear continuous head. MotionGPT: K=512 VQ codes beat continuous VAEs on reconstruction. Track-On: patch classification + bounded offset is the precise-and-robust combination.

### 2.5 Point trackers: how the refinement machinery is built

PIPs, TAPIR and CoTracker3 share one recipe: initialise a track (copy of query position, or a matching argmax), build per-frame tokens from *local correlation features + Fourier-embedded displacement + visibility/confidence*, run a temporal (and, in CoTracker, cross-track) transformer / MLP-Mixer, output **deltas** to position/visibility/confidence, iterate 4-6 times, and supervise every iterate with L1/Huber weighted gamma^(M-m), gamma=0.8. TAPIR's iteration ablation (AJ 41.6 / 55.0 / 61.3 / 61.6 / 60.4 for 0/1/4/5/6) is the strongest quantitative statement in this survey that *iterative delta refinement beats one-shot regression*, and CoTracker3's cross-track attention (+5.1 on occluded points) is the evidence that jointly decoding several trajectories helps. Track-On adds the classification-then-offset readout (w/o offset head AJ 64.9 -> 60.1) and shows the online, per-frame variant needs explicit memory (52.0 -> 64.9 AJ). None of these are single-image *forecasters*, but the token design and the loss are directly transplantable to a forecasting head where "frames" become future timesteps and correlation features become cross-attention to the fused feature map.

### 2.6 Language + trajectory via VLM tokens

LLARVA, HAMSTER / 3D HAMSTER, RT-Trajectory's VLM variant and HandsOnVLM all emit trajectories as *text tokens* (x,y[,d]) or via special tokens whose hidden state feeds a small decoder. The consistent finding is that predicting the trace as an *auxiliary* target helps the main task (LLARVA +15 % avg; HAMSTER 44.8 vs 36.6 % unguided; HandsOnVLM ADE 0.194 vs task-specific 0.209), and that adding a depth channel to the trace helps (3D HAMSTER 44.8 vs 38.8 %; RT-Trajectory 2.5D 67 vs 50 %). EgoMAN is the most relevant hybrid: three semantic waypoint tokens (start / contact / end) from the VLM, then a flow-matching motion expert fills in the 6-DoF trajectory; the FM expert is the largest single gain (ADE 0.273 -> 0.162) and the waypoint tokens add 0.161 -> 0.151 plus better OOD (HOT3D -27 % ADE).

## 3. What this says about our failure modes

* **Plain head jitter (roughness 0.07)**: expected. No paper in this field predicts 20 independent positions from one vector with only a position loss; every regressor either predicts residuals/velocities and adds a velocity loss (siMLPe), uses a basis (BEAST, FAST, our DCT) or an explicit second-difference penalty (RoboFlow4D).
* **DCT is smooth but "just a basis truncation"**: the literature agrees the basis itself is worth little (siMLPe: 1 mm) — the gains come from residual targets, velocity losses, scale decomposition and boundary constraints (BEAST pins c0). A DCT head with (a) the first sample pinned at zero, (b) a separate scale scalar and (c) a velocity loss is closer to BEAST/General Flow than what we have.
* **OOD squiggles while mask/type/axis stay right**: this is the signature of a head that has to *re-derive* the motion from a pooled feature that no longer carries it. Three remedies have evidence: decode from spatial features with per-timestep queries (all regression transformers; Gen2Act's motion-type 5 -> 30 %), condition/guide the trajectory on already-reliable structure (VidBot guidance ablation; FlowBot3D's flow is analytic in the joint parameters), and generative heads with best-of-K + cost-based selection (VidBot 85.6 vs 74.5 without selection; What-Happens-Next OOD transfer).

## 4. Ranked candidate head designs for SegAffordance

**1. Articulation-parameterised decoder + residual (structural, ~0 extra params).** Decode the trajectory *from* the predicted type / axis direction / hinge origin: for revolute, p(t) = R(axis, theta(t)) (p0 - o) + o - p0; for prismatic, p(t) = s(t) * axis; the head only predicts a monotone 1-D profile theta(t) or s(t) (e.g. 6 DCT coefficients of a cumulative-softplus curve, or a scale scalar times a fixed ease profile) and optionally a small bounded residual (tanh-limited, like Track-On's offset). Why it helps: a squiggle is *not representable*; OOD, the trajectory inherits whatever the (empirically robust) axis head gets right; the SF3D axis-consistency loss becomes an identity. Evidence: FlowBot3D/FlowBotHD's flow is exactly this analytic function of joint parameters and generalises across 166 unseen PartNet-Mobility instances; General Flow's shape/scale split (Table 4) is the same decomposition one level up; "Surface Keypoint Representation" ([2608.03158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03158)) notes revolute / prismatic / screw joints induce distinct, low-dimensional trajectory families. Cost: a few hundred parameters; one extra loss (profile monotonicity). Risk: the 2D-video datasets have no axis GT, so the residual branch must carry hand-motion deviations there — keep it bounded and L1-penalised.

**2. Query-token trajectory decoder with spatial cross-attention and iterative delta refinement (CoTracker/ATM/3PoinTr-style, ~2-5 M params).** Replace the pooled 512-d vector with 20 timestep tokens (+ one scale token) initialised from Fourier features of t, that cross-attend to the *unpooled* fused feature map at the contact point's neighbourhood and self-attend along time (2-3 layers, d=256); run M=3 delta updates with L1 loss weighted 0.8^(M-m) plus a velocity L1 (siMLPe). Why it helps: the head can look at the drawer's extent and the handle's surroundings instead of a global average, which is what ATM/Gen2Act/3PoinTr all rely on; delta refinement is the best-evidenced single mechanism in tracking (TAPIR 41.6 -> 61.3 AJ). Cost: ~2-5 M params, <1 ms; trivially compatible with the DCT readout (tokens can emit DCT coefficients instead of positions). Evidence: ATM/Tra-MoE (32 tokens, H=16, MSE), 3PoinTr (2 decoder blocks, L1, 64.9 % vs 35.75 % for 2D), CoTracker3 (4 delta iterations, Huber, gamma=0.8), Track-On (offset head delta-1px 45.5 vs 27.6).

**3. Small flow-matching trajectory head with best-of-K and axis-consistency guidance (VidBot/Affordance-FM/Forecasting-Motion-style, ~3-10 M params).** A 1D U-Net or 4-layer DiT that denoises the 20x3 (or 6x3 DCT) trajectory conditioned on the fused feature + contact point + predicted axis/type embedding, trained with rectified-flow velocity MSE (or L1); at inference sample K=8-16 with 1-8 Euler steps and select by the differentiable axis-consistency / smoothness cost (VidBot's cost-based selection, RoboFlow4D's second-difference penalty), or apply the cost as reconstruction guidance. Why it helps: multimodal 2D hand datasets (open left/right, push/pull) are exactly where regression averages into squiggles (FlowBotHD doors 23 -> 77 %); selection by structural cost removes the samples that contradict the axis head. Evidence: Affordance-FM 1.01 vs 4.91 cm and 1-step FM 1.03 cm; What-Happens-Next 10.99 vs 23.07; VidBot 85.6 vs 74.5 % without cost-based selection; Diff-IP2D vs AR regressors. Cost: 3-10 M params; K x steps forward passes of the head only (the backbone runs once), i.e. well under 10 ms on GPU with 1-4 steps.

**4. DCT/B-spline head done properly: pinned start, separate scale, velocity loss, discrete coefficient bins with continuous offset (BEAST/FAST/siMLPe, ~0.1 M params).** Keep the 6-coefficient basis (BEAST finds N=10 for 20 steps optimal; 6 with a pinned start is in the same regime), force p(0)=0 (BEAST's c0 pin), predict a log-scale scalar and *unit* shape (General Flow), add an L2/L1 velocity term (siMLPe 111.3 -> 109.4 mm), and optionally classify each coefficient into ~64 bins with a bounded offset (BEAST discrete +12.7 %; Track-On). Why it helps: closes the "basis truncation only" gap with the three ingredients that were shown to carry the gains; classification-plus-offset degrades more gracefully OOD than unconstrained regression. Cost: negligible. Evidence: BEAST Table (N=5/10/20 = 3.88/4.43/2.71; discrete vs continuous), siMLPe ablation, General Flow Table 4, FAST (binning 0 % vs DCT ~70 % at 50 Hz).

**5. Trajectory as auxiliary token target in a VLM/LLM-style decoder (LLARVA/HAMSTER/EgoMAN, tens of M params, only if we already have a language decoder).** Emit three semantic waypoint tokens (start / mid / end of motion, 3D) with a hybrid text+regression loss and let a small FM head interpolate (EgoMAN 0.161 -> 0.151 ADE from waypoints, best OOD). Why it helps: language grounding ("left drawer", "half open") flows into explicit waypoints before smoothing. Cost: the highest here; only worth it if a VLM backbone is adopted for other reasons. Evidence: LLARVA +15 %, HAMSTER 3D vs 2D 44.8 vs 38.8 %, EgoMAN ablation table.

Recommended order of experiments: (1) and (4) are near-free and orthogonal — do them first and re-measure roughness / OOD error; then (2) on top; (3) only if the 2D-video datasets are demonstrably multimodal (check whether best-of-K oracle error of the current head is far below its mean error — that gap is the signal that a generative head will pay off).
