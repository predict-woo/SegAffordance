# Survey: egocentric hand / interaction trajectory forecasting (2020-2026) and what it says about our jittery 20-point head

Date: 2026-09-10. Scope: how egocentric hand-trajectory forecasters parameterise, supervise and smooth their outputs, read against our head (plain MLP, ~800-d condition -> 20x3 relative points, 3D L2 on 3D data, index-matched 2D projection loss on hand tracks; rejected: truncated DCT readout, first-difference losses).

## Observations

1. Nobody in this literature uses a flat MLP emitting all future points from one pooled vector, except VRB (and VRB only emits 5 points). Everyone with 10+ points uses either a sequence decoder (autoregressive transformer / state space, per-step emission) or a diffusion / flow-matching denoiser over the whole trajectory. Per-step emission means neighbouring points share the same head and see each other; that is the main structural difference to our head.
2. Explicit smoothness terms are rare and, where ablated, motivated by generalisation not smoothness: USST's velocity-integration consistency gives its largest gain on unseen scenes (0.168 -> 0.120 m ADE); MADiff/Uni-Hand/MMTwin use angle+length losses on consecutive displacements (the same family as our rejected fdiff losses) and report only aggregate ADE/WDE gains. ObjectForesight and NIAF add acceleration/jerk penalties. No paper reports a roughness metric; "smooth" is asserted from figures.
3. Label hygiene is universal: OCT (Hermite spline), VRB (Savitzky-Golay, window 7), FHOI (linear interpolation of fingertips). All 2D tracks come from detectors warped through homographies and are smoothed before training. Our 2D path resamples raw hand-keypoint tracks to 20 points; if those tracks are rough, index-matched L2 will faithfully reproduce the roughness.
4. Uncertainty-aware regression (USST HAU/DRAU NLL) is the only supervised-regression paper here that explicitly handles noisy targets; diffusion/flow papers absorb noise in the generative prior.
5. Horizons are short (3-16 points) in almost all 2D work; 20 points is on the long end, where independent-output heads have the most room to zigzag.

## What applies to our head (ranked)

1. Time-conditioned shared head (implicit trajectory function). Replace the 800 -> 60 MLP by g(c, gamma(t_k)) -> R^3 evaluated at t_k = k/19, shared weights, gamma = Fourier features with a small number of octaves L (2-3) or a SIREN. Mechanism: neighbouring points are produced by the same continuous function of t, and the spectral bias of low-L Fourier/ReLU MLPs suppresses high-frequency wiggles without a hard cutoff (NeRMo explicitly frames L as the soft analogue of the DCT truncation; Neural Prior uses the temporal-coordinate MLP as its only smoothness prior; NIAF shows smoother curves and velocity profiles than discrete-waypoint heads). It is not a basis readout: any curve is representable if L is raised, and the fit stays end-to-end through the 2D projection loss. Side effects: 20 head evaluations per sample (negligible), one new hyperparameter L, risk of over-smoothing the contact onset if L is too small; the relative-to-first-point constraint is trivially imposed by subtracting g(c, gamma(0)). Evidence is from human-motion and robot-action prediction, not from egocentric hand forecasting.

2. Fix the target side: smooth the 2D hand tracks before resampling and use a heteroscedastic NLL. Savitzky-Golay (VRB) or Hermite/cubic spline (OCT) on the hand-keypoint track, then resample to 20 points; supervise with USST's HAU loss e^{-a_k}||r_k||^2 + a_k so the head can down-weight residual label noise per point rather than fit it. Mechanism: index-matched L2 to a rough track is a direct source of roughness; the NLL removes the incentive to chase outlier points. Side effects: needs a clamp on a_k to avoid variance inflation; changes the relative weighting of 3D vs 2D batches. Cheap: label preprocessing plus one extra output channel. Supported by OCT/VRB practice and USST's ablation, though neither measured roughness.

3. Velocity-integrated parameterisation with USST-style consistency. Emit 19 velocities v_k, decode p_k = sum_{i<=k} v_i (p_0 = 0), optionally also emit p directly and add gamma||p_0 + cumsum(v) - p_hat||^2 (gamma = 0.1 in USST). Mechanism: independent output noise on velocities becomes a random walk in position, whose second differences have variance 2 sigma^2 instead of 6 sigma^2 for independent points; the consistency term ties the two views. This is a parameterisation, not a loss on differences. Side effects: error accumulates towards the end point (FDE may worsen), so keep the direct branch for the endpoint; USST's gain (0.168 -> 0.120 m unseen) is a generalisation result, not a smoothness measurement, and VRB-style relative shifts on 5 points are the only egocentric precedent for pure cumsum decoding.

4. Alignment-tolerant 2D loss (soft-DTW or DILATE) instead of index-matched L2. Mechanism: the projected part-point curve and the hand-keypoint track are different physical points with different timing; forcing index k to index k rewards local wiggles that reduce point-to-point distance. Soft-DTW (gamma > 0) matches shape under time warps; DILATE adds a temporal-distortion penalty so the curve cannot collapse. EgoMAN already uses DTW as a metric on 6-DoF hand trajectories. Side effects: O(20x20) per sample (trivial), tuning gamma, possible time-drift of the prediction relative to the track (mitigated by DILATE or by anchoring endpoints with a small index-matched term). No egocentric hand paper trains with it; this is a transfer from time-series forecasting.

5. Diffusion / flow-matching head (what the field converged on). Diff-IP2D, MADiff, MMTwin, Uni-Hand, EgoH4, EgoMAN, SPOT all obtain smooth curves from an iterative denoiser over the whole trajectory. Mechanism: the denoiser sees all 20 points jointly and the training distribution of clean trajectories is smooth, so samples inherit it. Side effects: multi-step inference, multi-sample evaluation, and the 2D projection loss must be applied to the x0 estimate; Diff-IP2D still needed a sliding-window post-hoc smoothing variant, which shows the prior alone does not guarantee it. Not cheap; listed because it is the only approach with broad empirical support in this slice.

Not recommended from the literature: the rejected options are exactly what MADiff/Uni-Hand (angle+length losses) and the DCT-based human-motion line use, and no egocentric paper reports that they reduce roughness on 2D-supervised paths.
