Overnight report: the model goes RGB-only

What was built, what ran on the two pods, and what the numbers say. 2026-09-09, 01:30 to 06:40 local.

Commits f6536b3 … e0408b5 · experiments 20260909_hoi4d_2d_v2_rgb_scalefree, 20260909_sf3d_g19_dct_rgb_scalefree, 20260909_sf3d_g19_dct_rgb_scalefree_ft_hoi4d · spec docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-09-rgb-only-scale-free-trajectory-design.md

Bottom line. Depth is gone from the model, from the data loader and from every loss, and the trajectory head now predicts offsets in units of the anchor depth. On HOI4D this costs nothing and is in fact better on every metric. On SF3D the post-trained RGB-only model matches its depth counterpart on the headline metric (MA 30.07 vs 30.62, within single-seed noise) and beats it on detection, masks and axis. The one measurable price of no depth is the trajectory term's generalisation on SF3D, visible in the validation curve (0.48 vs 0.35) and as slightly shorter predicted sweeps in the panels.

1What was built

  1. A depth-free anchor for the 2D loss. TrajectoryProjectionLoss(anchor_source="unit") lifts the GT first pixel to depth exactly 1. Because projection ignores a global rescale, this is the same loss as before with the unknown scale factored out, and it never drops a row. Before, the loss sampled the depth map and silently dropped 80% of ARCTIC rows and 100% of EPIC rows.
  2. A scale-free trajectory head. model_params.trajectory_scale_free: the head emits Δ̃ = Δ/z₀. Right after the forward the trainer multiplies by a per-sample scale: nothing on the 2D datasets, the GT first-point depth on SF3D during training, the model's own z_p at test (test_trajectory_scale, with a gt_z0 oracle option). Every existing 3D loss and metric then sees metres as before.
  3. No depth in the input. use_depth: false (existing) plus a new load_depth: false on the dataset so the depth PNG is never decoded. An opt-in loader rule (finetune_slice_input_channels) can slice the depth columns off an old checkpoint's FPN convs, which is exactly the "concatenate zeros" model you asked about.
  4. Tests and smoke. 11 new unit tests (unit anchor exact for any z₀ with no depth map, invalid first point drops the row, scale sources, RGB-only forward with depth=None, loader slicing) plus 56 regression tests pass on the dev pod. fast_dev_run passed for the HOI4D, EPIC, ARCTIC and SF3D configs, and the SF3D test ran with both scale sources.
  5. Configs for every dataset: config/hoi4d_v2_rgb_scalefree.yaml (from the plain teacher-forcing recipe, as you asked), epic_v1_rgb_scalefree.yaml, arctic_v1_rgb_scalefree.yaml, and SF3D scratch + post-training variants. The two viz tools honour the new head.

Read the table in two pairs. Scratch pair: depth is worth about 1 MA, 4 PDet, 0.026 mIoU, 4° of axis and 5 cm of origin when the model starts from nothing. Post-trained pair: that cost disappears; the RGB model is within noise on MA and ahead on detection, mask and axis. The HOI4D init gives the RGB model +5.2 MA and +5.0 PDet over its own scratch (the depth pair gained +4.6 MA and lost 1.7 PDet), so the transfer survives without depth and is, if anything, cleaner.

Total validation loss. The RGB curves sit above the depth curves by roughly 0.1 throughout; the gap is almost entirely the trajectory term below.

The trajectory term, normalised, computed on GT z₀ · Δ̃ so it is the same quantity for both model kinds. Both RGB arms plateau near 0.48 while the depth arms keep improving to 0.35. Training loss reaches 0.009 for all four, so this is generalisation of the metric curve, not fitting.

Mask loss: near-identical with and without depth once the model is post-trained.

Why the two test passes were identical. I tested every SF3D model twice, with the predicted scale (z_p) and with the oracle scale (GT z₀). All reported metrics came out identical. MA is the type-plus-axis pass rate on IoU-matched samples and never reads the trajectory; the direction, roughness and 2D-shape metrics are scale-invariant; the 3D point metric reads z_p directly. So the test suite has no number that measures the metric trajectory error, and the scale question is currently answered only by the validation trajectory loss above. If the metric size of the sweep ever matters, that metric needs adding.

6What I would do next

  • The DCT teacher-forcing HOI4D arm under the new recipe as the SF3D init. Its depth counterpart is the 31.13 record; the DCT head bought +0.5 MA and +3 PDet on top of the plain arm with depth, and the same is likely here. One HOI4D hour plus one SF3D run.
  • First EPIC and ARCTIC runs. Configs are ready and smoke-tested, and this is what the whole change was for. ARCTIC has exact mocap z₀, so it could even feed the gt_z0 scale on a 2D dataset.
  • A metric trajectory-error test metric if the sweep size matters for anything downstream.
  • The trajectory generalisation gap (0.48 vs 0.35) is the thing to watch across all of the above.
