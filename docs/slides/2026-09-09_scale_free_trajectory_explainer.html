Scale-free 2D pretraining, scale from 3D

How to let the 2D datasets teach the shape of a trajectory without ever knowing depth, and let the 3D stage add the size afterwards as one number.

1The one fact everything rests on

Take an interaction point at depth z₀ in front of the camera, and a motion Δ in metres. The camera sees the motion as roughly Δ/z₀: a 10 cm pull at half a metre looks the same as a 20 cm pull at one metre. The projection formula makes this exact: scaling the whole 3D scene by any factor leaves the image unchanged.

Figure 1. Three metric motions, one image track. They differ in Δ and z₀ but share Δ/z₀.

So there are two separate quantities hiding inside "the trajectory": a shape Δ̃ = Δ/z₀ that the image determines completely, and a scale z₀ that the image cannot determine at all. Today the head predicts their product Δ, which is why the loss has to be handed z₀ from a depth map. The idea is to predict them separately.

2Today: the head predicts metres, so the loss needs a depth

Figure 2. The current chain. The metric head output only becomes projectable once z₀ is known, and z₀ comes from the depth map.

3Proposed: the head predicts the shape, the anchor sits at depth 1

Write the absolute curve as z₀·(r + Δₖ/z₀). Projecting z₀·(something) gives the same pixels as projecting (something). So if the head outputs Δ̃ₖ = Δₖ/z₀ directly, the loss can build the curve r + Δ̃ₖ, which is the same scene shrunk to anchor depth 1, and project that. No z₀ appears anywhere.

Figure 3. The 2D path after the change. The anchor's pixel is known from the data; its depth is fixed to 1 by convention. EPIC and ARCTIC supervise every record.

Worked example. A drawer pull seen head-on. The hand starts at pixel (300, 260) and ends at (300, 320) in a 512-pixel image with f = 500 px. The 2D track says the point moved 60 px.

The head learns to output 0.12 for this image, whichever kitchen it came from. That is a statement about the image, not about the room.

4Where the metres come back

Everything downstream that wants metric trajectories, the 3D trajectory loss on SF3D, the closed-form quadratics, the articulation consistency terms, the DCT head, the MA metric, keeps working unchanged. It just receives z₀·Δ̃ instead of Δ, where z₀ is supplied by whoever knows it:

Figure 4. One multiply separates the two roles. The shape is trained wherever there are 2D tracks; the scalar is trained wherever there is depth.

Why this is more than a workaround. An RGB network cannot see metres; it can see Δ/z₀. Asking the head for metric offsets was asking it to also guess depth, which is why the recipe had to feed it depth through the anchor. Asking it for Δ/z₀ is asking for what is actually in the picture. The one number it cannot see, z₀, is handled by the one head built for depth.
