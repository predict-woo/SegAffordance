Gen-9: the new baseline · the supervision ablation (08-15)

Two things happened on one split. First, gen-9 established the standing 59k close-up split (mask ≥ 0.1% + 5% edge margin) as the project's reference denominator — every generation since trains on it. Second, three arms trained on that identical split answered a design question the architecture had assumed since gen-6: does jointly supervising trajectory and articulation actually help either one? Answer: yes, in both directions.

Everything else identical across arms: same 59,174-record split, same cache, 30 epochs, milestones [24, 28], seed 42, frozen CLIP, RTX PRO 4500 (~1 h, ≈ $0.9 per arm). Heads are removed from the architecture, not merely unsupervised — arm C even drops origin_uv from the condition vector. Locked decision: the interaction point counts as static grounding, so its full pipeline stays in every arm; only the 20-point motion trajectory counts as trajectory supervision.

Conclusion (recorded 08-15): joint supervision wins both ways — each branch is the other's regularizer, at a small, measured mask cost. The gen-6 two-branch design is validated; every subsequent generation keeps both supervision paths.
Attribution caveat (accepted in the spec): arm A differs from B and C by two mechanisms — the extra supervision signal AND the L_pp consistency coupling. These arms cannot separate co-training from the consistency loss; a joint-minus-L_pp arm is a recorded follow-up, not part of this experiment.
