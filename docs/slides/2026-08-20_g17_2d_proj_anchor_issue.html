Why the 2D Reprojection Error Stayed Terrible

The 2D-only arm optimized exactly the metric you care about — projected predicted 3D curve vs the GT 2D track — and still ended WORSE than a model that predicts zero motion. Two stacked problems explain it: the metric is mostly measuring where the curve is pinned (the anchor), and the loss's own gradients destroyed the thing that does the pinning.

2 · Why the anchor died: the loss's own gradient path

So the causality is circular and self-defeating: the loss needs a good anchor to measure shape → its own amplified gradients destroy the features that place the anchor → the anchor offset dominates the loss → the trajectory head gets almost no usable shape signal.

Even the best 3D-supervised model scores ~0.10–0.12 shape error against a 0.21 motion scale — its arcs ride the tracks direction-wise but over/under-shoot in extent, and this metric charges every uv of that. "Learned it somewhat" on this metric looks like ~0.10, not ~0.01. The 2D-only arm's real shortfall is the anchor column.

4 · The fix that falls out of the diagram

Cut the red path: detach the anchor.

Stop gradients at the anchor (both point_uv and the sampled z) inside the projection loss. The term then teaches ONLY trajectory shape — and point placement keeps its own clean, direct 2D supervision (heatmap + coord), which is how g17 gets its 0.095 point error. The depth-edge gradient bomb is defused entirely; the anchor still improves, just via the right teacher.

Drop the ×30–50 amplifier.

Back to plain masked MSE (the twist-era form that coexisted with healthy masks), or cap the normalized ratio. The rot/trans scale imbalance the normalization targeted is much milder in bounded uv space than it was in meters.

Track what you actually care about.

Add test/traj_proj2d_err (+ the anchor/shape split) to the standard test pass — for every arm, not just 2D ones. It would also have exposed g17's own 0.15 headroom a week ago. The probe (tools/diag_proj2d.py) already computes it.
