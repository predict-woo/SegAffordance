Point refinement: which pixel do you sample?

The plan: keep the heatmap's soft-argmax as the coarse point xc, sample the decoded feature map there, and let a small head predict an offset to a refined point xr. The question: the feature you sampled describes where you started, not where you ended — and the depth head ẑp wants appearance at the final point. Where do you cut this loop?

1 · The refinement idea (no issue yet)

Soft-argmax over a broad heatmap lands at the blob's mass center (xc), not the sharp handle. The offset head reads the appearance at xc and nudges the point to xr. So far so good — until you ask which pixel the depth head should look at.

2 · The chicken-and-egg

The feature that justifies the move is always one step behind the move itself. You can never hold "the feature at the final point" before committing to the final point — so you must decide where to stop sampling.

3 · Three places to cut the loop

A · One pass + one re-sample — recommended

Each head reads the best feature available at the moment it runs. The only location/feature mismatch left is inside the offset head itself — bounded by the ≤ ~13 px offset cap, and that head's whole job is "look here, say which way to shift".

B · Iterate K times

Legitimate technique (iterative error feedback in pose estimation) when corrections are large. Our correction budget is a dozen pixels — the second iteration would be refining noise.

C · Single sample for both heads

Depth boundaries are sharp exactly where refinement matters (a handle against a dark oven interior — centimeters in uv, a meter in depth). Reading depth appearance at the pre-move pixel throws away the refinement's benefit for ẑ_p.

Recommendation: option A. The forward becomes: heatmap → xc → sample@xc → Δ → xr → sample@xr → ẑp → lift. Both grid_samples are differentiable, so L_coord / L_point_3d train the offset head end-to-end; L_point_map keeps supervising the raw heatmap so the coarse localizer never degrades.
