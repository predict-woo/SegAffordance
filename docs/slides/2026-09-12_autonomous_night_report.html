2. The joint decoder: it works, and the SF3D-side loss decides the numbers

The decoder renders the trajectory from the model's own type, axis, origin and point heads plus a learned arc length (revolute: angle = length / radius with the radius detached and clamped at π; prismatic: length × direction). Hand-video batches train it through the unit-anchor projection loss with GT-routed type; SF3D batches see no rendered curve at all, only a closed-form loss on the articulation parameters. Four SF3D-side losses were tried on the otherwise identical joint4 recipe.

Reading.

  • The decoder alone (L2 2π) is a sound replacement for the head. Same MA as joint4, far better origin and radius (the 2π quadratic's 3 : 1 radial weighting plus the projection loss reaching the origin through the arc), masks kept, smooth by construction. Its cost is direction accuracy (96 → 91): the curve inherits every axis-sign error, whereas the free head had learned the sign better than the axis head.
  • Adding an axis term with a radius-scaled partner hurts. Both anchor-carrying variants (L2 + axis, H1 + axis) raise hinge flips from 14 % to 19 % and lose masks or MA; the +axis variant's validation axis term is identical to the base's (0.279 vs 0.281), so the anchor added no axis information under the decoder — the 2D projection loss already supplies sign through the arc — and only re-balanced the trunk.
  • The shape-designed loss is the right SF3D side. Its axis term is the same 1 − cos, but inside a loss whose other term is also scale-free in the lever, and it drops the flips instead of raising them. MA 36.36 comes from the core of the distribution: the mean all-axis error moves only 25.3 → 24.3, but five more percent of the test set falls under 10°. The costs are the origin (+4 cm) and masks (−0.014), where the 2π L2 base is better.

Three things the master formula predicted and the runs confirmed: halving the L2 leak (π/2 → 2π) is worth +2.7 MA and a large origin gain but cannot fix sign (L2 alone has no scale-free axis term at any sweep); the p = 0, ρ = 0 loss gives the best masks, origin and radius of the family; and sign needs the strict curvature condition — at 2 : 1 the flipped axis has one flat direction and flips went up, at 3 : 1 they fell to 13.0 and MA reached 31.90, at the cost of origin and masks. The 3 : 1 + heavier radius arm (running) probes that trade.

4. Two structural findings

Sign under the decoder. On the arm-B config the direct axis loss was worth +1.5 MA (no other sign source). Under the joint decoder the hand-video projection loss supplies the axis sign through the rendered arc, and the two anchor-carrying variants both showed +5 hinge-flip points with no axis-term improvement on validation. The working hypothesis: the hand track fixes the sign relative to the observed motion by the right-hand rule, the direct loss pulls toward SF3D's canonical sign, and where these differ per instance the tail of flipped hinges grows. The seed replicate (running) says how much of the 14 → 19 is noise; the cf_frame variant, whose axis term sits inside a fully scale-free loss, moves the other way (13.4).

The arc-length head is a hand-video quantity. Trained only by the projection loss, it predicts ~0.21 m sweeps on SF3D for hinges and slides alike, 0.3× the writer's constants and uncorrelated with them (probe over 400 val samples, tools/decoder_length_probe.py). Expected: SF3D never supervises length. No test metric reads it; renders can use the writer constants (trajectory_decoder_length writer).

5. The chain under the decoder (stage 1 done)

The 2D multi-source arm with the decoder is clearly weaker than the DCT-head arm on masks and detection (union 0.626 / 72.9 vs 0.715 / 83.8): with no 3D data the projection loss has to invent the articulation from the hand track and drives the trunk through the axis/origin/point heads. Whether this init still transfers to SF3D (stage 2, running; compare the DCT chain's 32.80) is the open question. Note its SF3D stage carries the direct axis loss, decided before the l2anchor result.

The record replicates and the noise is calibrated. The base recipe moves by 2 MA, 4 cm of origin and 0.02 mIoU between seeds, so single-seed differences below that are inconclusive — which retires the earlier "origin big win" reading of the base and softens the anchor-variant readings (their +5 flips remain suggestive). Against that noise, cf_frame sits at 36.0–36.7 across three runs versus a base mean of 32.3: a +4 MA effect. At seed 7 it also holds the best all-axis (22.5°), signed-all (29.6°), hinge-flip (9.5 %) and PDet (23.9) numbers of the whole project; at 3 : 1 the all-flip rate drops to 7.8 % and the matched axis to 16.1° at a mask cost. The heavier radius weight on the arm-B config did not buy the origin back (0.270 vs 0.262) and blurred the axis. The decoder chain transfers poorly (28.8): under the decoder, joint training beats the two-stage chain.

8. Suggested next steps

  1. cf_frame is the default SF3D side of the joint decoder recipe (record replicated). The axis : phase ratio is the sign-vs-mask knob (2 : 1 all-round, 3 : 1 sign); the log-radius weight is not a lever (0.30 hurt). Try 2.5 : 1 and a third seed of the record.
  2. Hand-source masks: every decoder arm loses HOI4D masks (0.676 → ~0.6). Candidates: lower projection weight, or detach the depth heads (z_p, z_q) inside the decoder on 2D batches so the projection loss trains axis and length only.
  3. Direction accuracy: the decoder's 91–92 vs the head's 96 is the axis head's sign; ARCTIC's GT axes under the closed-form loss are the untouched lever for hinges.
  4. Seeds: one seed per arm everywhere; the two replicates running are the first calibration.
