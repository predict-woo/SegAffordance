Why our articulation model predicted
giant radii and frozen trajectories
— and how we fixed it

SegAffordance · SF3D twist arm · diagnosis and gen-4 redesign, Aug 2026

One sentence: the network was trained with losses whose optimum under ambiguity is the average of the possible answers — and for articulated motion, the average of the possible answers is a physically absurd motion. We replaced the loss geometry (body-frame metric) and made the model commit to one answer (winner-takes-all hypotheses).

The symptomsMeasured on the trained gen-3 model (800 validation samples)

0.295

median predicted ‖ω‖ on revolute joints — ground truth is 1.0. 77% of rotating parts decode as sliding.

10×

median radius inflation, one-sided: 96% of predictions have a radius larger than GT (0.25 m → 1.4 m). This is the "too-flat arcs" in the renders.

0.0182

trajectory loss after 16 epochs — the baseline of predicting no motion at all scores 0.0181. The trajectory head learned nothing.

Also explained: emergent motion-type accuracy ~60% (it's the ω-collapse, not a type problem), direction accuracy ~71%.

Root cause 1 — mode averagingMSE predicts the mean; the mean of two valid motions is an invalid motion

  • When the image doesn't determine the answer (door vs drawer, swing direction, hinge side), the MSE-optimal prediction is the probability-weighted average of the possibilities.
  • In twist coordinates: average of revolute (‖ω‖=1) and prismatic (ω=0) → ‖ω‖ = P(rot) < 1. The decoded axis position divides by ‖ω‖², so the hinge flies away → radius inflates as ~1/P(rot). Always larger, never smaller.
  • Same for trajectories: mean of "arc left" and "arc right" is zero motion — exactly what we measured.
  • Verified: a toy network trained with our exact loss on ambiguous data lands exactly on this average; the old branched (classify-then-regress) design does not — matching "the old implementation was fine".

Root cause 2 — mispriced errorsThe loss charged almost nothing for exactly the errors that mattered

The 6-vector MSE ‖Δω‖² + ‖Δv‖² is secretly a physical metric — of a body sitting at the camera origin, ~2 m away from the object whose motion we predict. Priced in that gauge:

errorcost (relative to a full sign flip)
axis 0.5 m too far away (radius 3× wrong)1.4%
the observed ‖ω‖ collapse to 0.32.7%
axis direction tilt of 6°0.23%  (fine — quadratic small-angle law)
  • The loss surface is nearly flat along radius-controlling directions while the decoded geometry is hyperbolically sensitive there — weight decay and noise park ω low for free.
  • The auxiliary consistency losses can't push back: they compare directions only (scale-free), and on a 15° arc they cannot tell radius 0.18 m from 0.61 m.

Fix 1 — body-frame metricPrice errors by how wrongly they move the object

L(Δξ)  =  ‖Δω × p₀ + Δv‖²  +  ρ² ‖Δω‖²

  • The classical rigid-body kinetic-energy metric on se(3), with the body placed at the observed element point p₀ (ρ = 0.25 m part scale). First term = the velocity error at the object, in m².
  • Gauge-invariant (the old loss changed if you moved the camera origin; this one can't), still convex, no motion-type branches, sign-sensitive.

Radius-relevant directions gain ~40–100× relative gradient; direction pricing keeps its healthy ∝δ law. Overall loss scale is preserved (measured 0.95×), so no re-tuning cascade.

Fix 2 — winner-takes-all hypothesesLet the model tell four stories and commit to one

  • Each hypothesis is a bundled (twist, trajectory) — one coherent story. Per sample, only the bundle closest to ground truth trains on it (softmin-annealed so no hypothesis dies early); a selector picks one at inference.
  • No hypothesis is ever forced to explain "left" and "right" at once → averaging is structurally impossible. Theory: hypotheses converge onto the modes of the answer distribution (Voronoi quantization), never between them. Standard in motion forecasting (aMCL/aWTA, 2024–25).
  • Toy replication of our failure: MSE head → ‖ω‖ = 0.49, radius 3×; WTA winner → ‖ω‖ > 0.95, radius ≈ GT — on identical ambiguous data.

StatusImplemented, tested, training

  • Diagnosis is measured, not conjectured: 800-sample checkpoint study, closed-form + trained-toy reproductions, and an intervention test (feeding the true motion type changed nothing → sign/geometry ambiguity dominates, so only mode commitment can fix it).
  • Both fixes implemented behind config flags; 109 unit tests including: gauge invariance, the pricing table as regression assertions, winner-only gradients, and the commitment-vs-averaging toy as an automated test.
  • Trajectory supervision re-weighted (0.5 → 4.0) — meaningful only now that the term is reducible; previously it idled at ~1% of the loss against a hedge-capped optimum.
  • gen-4 run in flight (RTX 4090 pod, ~4 h). Pre-registered acceptance: revolute ‖ω‖ ≈ 1 (was 0.295), radius ratio ≈ 1 (was 10×), trajectory loss well below the 0.018 zero-motion baseline, type/direction accuracy up.
