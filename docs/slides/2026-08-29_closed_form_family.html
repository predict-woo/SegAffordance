The closed-form family: subtracting our way to a record

The continuous trajectory loss has exactly two terms. We ran the pair, then removed one thing at a time — and the smallest configuration set the all-time articulation record. This page explains what the terms are, what each one turned out to do, and why removing one helped.

1What the closed form actually is

Trajectory co-training helps articulation because of loss geometry (the mechanism study's verdict). Taking that seriously, we computed the N→∞ limit of "decode the screw curve from the predicted articulation, compare to GT": the integrals collapse into two named quadratics on the articulation heads — no trajectory head, no sampled points, zero parameters. Those two terms are the whole closed form:

The two closed-form terms. Position compares the curves' places; derivative (H¹) compares their motions. Config knobs: closed_form_trajectory_weight (position) and closed_form_velocity_weight (derivative). The H¹ term is exactly the N→∞ limit of the fdiff family's velocity loss — that is the sense in which "H¹-only" = "the closed-form fdiff". On prismatic rows BOTH terms collapse to 2(1−cos) on the slide direction — the sign-aware axis loss itself.

Alongside these, the classical per-branch losses stay on — in particular the direct axis 1−cos term (vae_weight) and the 3D origin/point losses. Those three knobs — position, derivative, axis anchor — are what the three runs below toggle.

4Why did removing a term help? The job map

The three runs triangulate what each loss term is actually for — each job is read off the metric that moves when that term is toggled:

Each removal experiment isolates one term's contribution. The surprising part: position and derivative are not additive — the position gradient partially re-blurs what H¹ sharpens (closedform's matched axis was 22.3° vs 16.6° without it), because place-matching tolerates axis error whenever origin error compensates, while motion-matching does not.
The story in one sentence: the whole benefit of trajectory supervision distills into match the motion, not the place — one H¹ quadratic on the articulation heads (plus the classical sign anchor) now beats every trajectory-head recipe we ever ran, with the best masks of any trajectory-supervised arm as a side effect.
What died: "the position term is the important half" (it was the MA deadweight); "the direct axis loss might be redundant" (provably identifiable without it, but its sign-anchoring is worth 1.5 MA — cf_noaxis).
What's open (parked, not commissioned): a small position weight (0.1–0.2) to chase the MA record and the 0.250 origin record in one run; the Gram/Θ sweep; H¹-only + DCT head as the distilled gen-22 (masks); a seed replicate of the record; the angle-term hybrid (sampled, no closed form exists) for the last of the matched-axis gap.
