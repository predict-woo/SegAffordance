Does the ablation transfer to the fdiff family?

The DCT-family results — joint-training wins, and the "redundant label = loss geometry" mechanism — re-tested on the fdiff family (plain trajectory head + first-difference losses). Two questions, two new runs, two reused corners.

SegAffordance · experiments of 2026-08-27 → 28 · new arms: 20260827_sf3d_supabl3_traj_fdiff (C_f), 20260827_sf3d_andec_fdiff (decode+fdiff) · reused: supabl2 arm B (articulation-only is family-independent — fdiff dies with the trajectory head, so there is only ONE no-trajectory model) and fdnolpp (joint, no consistency terms) · spec: 2026-08-27-fdiff-family-ablation-design.md

Q1Is joint training better than either path alone? Yes — on both sides.

B — articulation-only C_f — trajectory-only joint (fdnolpp)
Both coupling directions favor joint training on the fdiff family. The right panel is the surprise: on the DCT family, articulation→trajectory measured ~zero (94.36 vs 94.26 — "the coupling is asymmetric"). With fdiff's direction-sensitive losses in play, articulation supervision gives the trajectory a measurable assist. The asymmetry survives — one side is 5× the other — but the weak side is no longer nil. Smoothness is untouched by articulation (roughness 0.0497 vs 0.0499): it belongs entirely to the fdiff losses.

Q2Does the mechanism verdict transfer? Yes — ~89% loss geometry.

Same discriminator as before: delete the trajectory head, decode the curve deterministically from the predicted articulation parameters (zero new parameters), and — new for this family — apply the fdiff first-difference losses to the decoded curve.

B — no trajectory decode+fdiff (0 params) joint (head)
The zero-parameter decode recovers 6.3 of the 7.1-point MA gap (DCT family: 75%) — and outright beats the joint arm on flip rate, origin (0.259 vs 0.266), and masks (0.257 vs 0.246). The head's edge is confined to matched-axis sharpness and radius (0.113 vs 0.135). Bonus: fdiff geometry composes with the decode parameter-free — vs the plain decode of two days ago it improves matched axis −1.1° and rot flips −2.4.
The mechanism conclusion is family-robust. On both trajectory representations, the articulation value of the "redundant" trajectory label is the loss geometry — a dense, jointly-constrained, GT-anchored objective over the same quantities — recoverable with zero parameters and no gradient routing through shared features. The fraction is, if anything, larger on the family whose losses carry more geometry (89% vs 75%).

✗The one thing that did NOT transfer: the mask ordering inverts

Same experiment design, opposite ordering. On DCT, trajectory-only had the best masks of all arms; on fdiff it loses to articulation-only, and the joint arm drops to 0.246. Every fdiff arm's masks sit at ~0.24–0.25 while DCT arms hold 0.265+.
Revised story: "trajectory supervision is trunk-friendly" — the account given after the DCT ablation — was a DCT-head property, not a trajectory property. The smooth 6-coefficient basis filters the gradients flowing back through the GT-mask-pooled features; the plain head + first-difference losses send harsher signals, and the trunk pays. Consistent with the decode arms too: the fdiff decode's mask dip vs arm B is milder than the DCT-era decode's, and it no longer undercuts the joint arm.
