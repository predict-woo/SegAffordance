The continuous limit of the trajectory loss

What happens to our decoded-trajectory loss when the number of sampled points \(N \to \infty\)? Answer: the sampling disappears entirely — the loss becomes a closed-form metric on the articulation parameters themselves, with each term interpretable.

SegAffordance theory note · follows the trajectory-mechanism study (2026-08-27/28) · conventions: relative frame, GT-matched parameterization

1Setup

The decode maps articulation parameters to a curve as a function of the motion variable \(\theta\). In the relative frame (curve starts at the origin):

Rotation — lever \(\mathbf r = (I - \hat n \hat n^\top)(p_0 - q)\), tangent \(\mathbf t = \hat n \times \mathbf r\), sweep \(\Theta = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\):

\[ p(\theta) = (\cos\theta - 1)\,\mathbf r + \sin\theta\,\mathbf t, \qquad \theta \in [0, \Theta] \]

Translation — unit direction \(\hat d\), extent \(L\):

\[ p(\theta) = \theta L\, \hat d, \qquad \theta \in [0, 1] \]

The \(N\)-point training loss is a mean of squared errors at uniform \(\theta\) samples, normalized per-row by the GT curve's energy. That mean is a Riemann sum, so as \(N \to \infty\) it converges (at \(O(1/N^2)\) on these smooth integrands) to

\[ \mathcal L \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\Theta \bigl\lVert p(\theta) - p^*(\theta) \bigr\rVert^2 \, d\theta} {\displaystyle\int_0^\Theta \bigl\lVert p^*(\theta) \bigr\rVert^2 \, d\theta} \;=\; \frac{\lVert \Phi(\text{pred}) - \Phi(\text{gt}) \rVert^2_{L^2}} {\lVert \Phi(\text{gt}) \rVert^2_{L^2}} \]

where \(\Phi\) is the decode viewed as a map from parameters into the function space \(L^2([0,\Theta];\mathbb R^3)\). So the continuous loss is the \(L^2\) distance between the curves as functions of \(\theta\) — a relative error, dimensionless by the normalization.

Why "as functions" matters. This is not a distance between the curves' images (point sets), like Chamfer or Hausdorff. A reversed arc has the same image but is a different function of \(\theta\) — which is exactly why axis sign survives in this loss and is invisible to any geometry-only distance.

2The integral closes — infinite points for free

Both curves are linear in the fixed basis \(\{\cos\theta - 1,\ \sin\theta\}\), so with residuals \(\Delta\mathbf r = \mathbf r - \mathbf r^*\) and \(\Delta\mathbf t = \mathbf t - \mathbf t^*\):

\[ p(\theta) - p^*(\theta) = (\cos\theta - 1)\,\Delta\mathbf r + \sin\theta\,\Delta\mathbf t \]

and the numerator needs only three scalar integrals over \([0, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]\):

\[ \int (\cos\theta-1)^2 = \tfrac{3\pi}{4} - 2, \qquad \int \sin^2\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{4}, \qquad \int (\cos\theta-1)\sin\theta = -\tfrac12 \]

Rotation, closed form:

\[ \int_0^{\pi/2}\!\lVert p - p^* \rVert^2\,d\theta = \Bigl(\tfrac{3\pi}{4}-2\Bigr)\lVert\Delta\mathbf r\rVert^2 + \tfrac{\pi}{4}\,\lVert\Delta\mathbf t\rVert^2 - \Delta\mathbf r \cdot \Delta\mathbf t \]

A fixed positive-definite quadratic form — the Gram matrix \(\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} 0.356 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.785 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr]\) of the two basis functions (\(\det \approx 0.03 > 0\)) — acting on the radial residual \(\Delta\mathbf r\) and the tangential residual \(\Delta\mathbf t\). The tangential term, which carries the axis sign (\(\mathbf t = \hat n \times \mathbf r\) flips with \(\hat n\)), is weighted \(\approx 2.2\times\) the radial one at a 90° sweep.

Translation, closed form:

\[ \int_0^{1}\!\lVert \theta L(\hat d - \hat d^*) \rVert^2\,d\theta = \tfrac{L^2}{3}\,\lVert \hat d - \hat d^* \rVert^2 = \tfrac{2L^2}{3}\,\bigl(1 - \cos\angle(\hat d, \hat d^*)\bigr) \]

— i.e. the continuous prismatic trajectory loss is exactly the sign-aware \(1-\cos\) axis loss, rescaled. In the limit, the "redundant label" story compresses to: for translation it literally is the axis loss; for rotation it is a genuinely different quadratic coupling axis \(\wedge\) origin \(\wedge\) point through \((\mathbf r, \mathbf t)\).

3What it is, conceptually: a pullback metric

\(\Phi\) embeds articulation parameters into curve space; the loss is the \(L^2\) metric pulled back through \(\Phi\):

\[ d_{\text{artic}}(x, y) \;=\; \lVert \Phi(x) - \Phi(y) \rVert_{L^2} \]

"Trajectory supervision" is thus: replace the hand-picked product of per-head losses with the metric induced by the physical motion itself. Our empirical mechanism finding — the zero-parameter decode recovers ~90% of the trajectory benefit — restates as: the pullback metric is better-conditioned for learning than the ad-hoc one. And the nonconvexity localizes cleanly: the loss is exactly quadratic in \((\mathbf r, \mathbf t)\); everything hard lives in the chart \((\hat n, q, p_0) \mapsto (\mathbf r, \mathbf t)\).

4The first-difference losses have a limit too: Sobolev

First differences divided by the step converge to derivatives, so the velocity / segment-direction / segment-length losses converge to functionals of \(p'(\theta)\) — the loss family becomes a Sobolev \(H^1\) metric (position + first-derivative matching); \(k\)-th differences would give \(H^k\). The derivative term also closes for our curves:

\[ \int_0^{\pi/2}\!\lVert p' - p^{*\prime} \rVert^2\,d\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigl(\lVert\Delta\mathbf r\rVert^2 + \lVert\Delta\mathbf t\rVert^2\Bigr) - \Delta\mathbf r \cdot \Delta\mathbf t \]

Note the re-weighting: the derivative term treats radial and tangential residuals equally (\(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) each), where the position term favored the tangential one. That is a tidy account of why the first-difference losses and the point loss proved complementary rather than redundant in the experiments — they are different quadratic forms on the same residuals.

5Practical corollaries

corollaryconsequence
Replace the 20-point decode with the closed-form quadratic zero discretization error, no \(N\) to choose, cheaper — "infinite points" computed exactly
The sweep extent \(\Theta\) becomes a principled knob the Gram coefficients are functions of \(\Theta\): as \(\Theta \to 0\) the metric collapses to pure instantaneous-velocity (tangent/sign) supervision; larger \(\Theta\) grows the radial/origin weight
Term-by-term interpretability the loss decomposes into named residuals (radial, tangential/sign) with explicit weights — diagnosable per sample
Honest caveat: at \(N = 20\) the quadrature error of the sampled loss is already tiny (\(O(1/N^2)\) on smooth trig integrands), so the closed form is an elegance-and-analysis win more than a performance one. Its real value is making the loss interpretable in articulation space — and revealing that the prismatic case was the axis loss all along.
